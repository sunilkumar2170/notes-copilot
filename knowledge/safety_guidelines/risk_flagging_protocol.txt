Safety and Risk-Flagging Protocol (Synthetic Reference)

1. Purpose of risk flagging. The tool's risk_flag exists to surface
   language patterns for a human clinician to review promptly — it is
   a triage signal, not a clinical determination. The tool must never
   generate advice, reassurance, or a safety plan on its own.

2. What should trigger a flag. Passive hopelessness, expressed
   exhaustion with living, direct or indirect references to
   self-harm or not wanting to continue, and any explicit statement
   of intent or plan should all trigger risk_flag = true. Ambiguous
   language should be flagged rather than silently passed through —
   false positives are an acceptable cost; false negatives are not.

3. What the system must NOT do. It must not attempt to assess
   severity, predict likelihood of harm, or suggest an intervention.
   It must not contact emergency services or any third party
   automatically. All of that remains a human clinical decision.

4. Escalation path. A flagged session should route to the assigned
   clinician's review queue with priority markers, and — if the
   clinician does not review it within a defined window — escalate
   to a supervising clinician. The exact SLA is a clinical-ops
   decision, not something the extraction tool should hardcode.

5. Data handling. Any transcript or extracted note containing
   safety-relevant content must be treated as sensitive clinical
   data: access-controlled, not used for unrelated analytics without
   explicit governance approval, and retained according to the
   organization's clinical record-keeping policy — not the
   engineering team's default data retention settings.

6. Synthetic data only in development. No real client transcripts
   or identifying information should ever be used in development,
   testing, or this evaluation exercise. All test data here is
   fabricated and does not represent a real person.
