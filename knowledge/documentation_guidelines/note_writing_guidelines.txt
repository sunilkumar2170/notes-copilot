Clinical Note Documentation Guidelines (Synthetic Reference)

1. Neutral language only. Notes should describe what was said and
   observed, not interpret intent or diagnose. Use phrases like
   "client reported" or "client stated" rather than "client is."

2. Follow-up actions must be concrete and attributable. Every action
   item should specify who is responsible (client or clinician) and,
   where possible, a rough timeframe.

3. Mood and affect should be recorded using the client's own words
   where available, supplemented with clinician observation clearly
   labeled as such (e.g. "client appeared low energy" vs "client
   reported feeling low energy").

4. Recurring themes across sessions should be noted explicitly when
   a topic has come up more than once, so patterns are visible to
   whoever reads the chart next — including another clinician
   covering a session.

5. Avoid summarizing so briefly that clinical nuance is lost, but
   avoid verbatim transcription — a note should be usable by another
   clinician who was not present in the session.

6. Any safety-relevant content must be documented factually and
   precisely: what was said, what was asked in response, and what
   the client's stated intent or denial was. Do not soften or
   omit this information for brevity.
